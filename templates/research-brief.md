# Research Brief

Use this structure to prepare source material for image generation. Keep the brief concise and source-grounded.

## Source Summary

Write three to six bullets describing what the source is, what it does, and why it matters.

## Viewer Takeaway

Write one sentence that the viewer should understand after looking at the image.

## Audience

Name the viewer: developer, technical decision maker, operator, founder, investor, customer, or another clear audience.

## Source-Backed Claims

List the claims the image may communicate. Keep them grounded in the source. Do not add metrics, dates, product names, architecture, commands, or outcomes that are not present in the source.

## Visualizable Relationships

List process steps, cause-and-effect relationships, architecture blocks, timelines, comparisons, taxonomies, or workflows that can become visual structure.

## Best-Fit Output Lane

State `social`, `infographic`, or `mixed-pack`, and give one sentence explaining why.

## Suggested Visual Grammar

Pick one grammar or archetype: hero metaphor, editorial poster, pipeline, layered stack, timeline, matrix, system map, command map, comparison, taxonomy, or dependency graph.

## Required Labels

List exact labels using P0, P1, and P2 priority tiers.

## Text Budget Risks

Name any labels, filenames, commands, or claims that may be too long or too numerous for the requested crop.

## Sensitivity Notes

List any sensitive source material that was skipped or requires user approval before it can appear in prompts or generated images.

## Risks, Caveats, Assumptions, And Unknowns

List only source-supported risks or clearly marked assumptions. Omit weak guesses.

## Source Notes

List source files, URLs, or passages the prompt should respect.
